01 About

I've been building software for over {{YEARS_CODING}} years — my first public projects went up on GitHub in {{FIRST_REPO_YEAR}}, and my oldest still-maintained one, the Telegram Support Bot, has been running since 2017. For the past five-plus years I've done it professionally: in logistics integration by day, and as the developer of open-source tools people actually use on the side.

At NEOZO I've spent three years in one continuous engagement in the carrier and transport-management space of an international logistics provider — delivering applications spanning bookings, sailing schedules and shipment status processing, along with shared libraries used across projects. A few months in, I took on the tech lead role for a team of six developers, and I've owned the architecture decisions in my project scope ever since. The most interesting problems live at the edges: heterogeneous data sources with unreliable quality, matching without unambiguous identifiers, and integrating modern microservices into a grown legacy landscape.

In parallel I build and commercially distribute my own developer tools — including the WSL Manager (Microsoft Store) and a support bot that also runs as a hosted service. 60+ releases, several major migrations and an active community have taught me more about architecture decisions and their long-term consequences than any whiteboard. I occasionally write about technical topics at senpai.club.

My background is in computer engineering (FH Aachen University of Applied Sciences, bachelor thesis graded 1.0 on the architecture modernization of a production system).
